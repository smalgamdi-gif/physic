## B. Theory analysis per chapter (تحليل الجانب النظري)

### Chapter 1 — Units and Dimension (S01, BK p.13–21)
1. **Sections:** 1. Physical quantities · 2. Units · 3. Conversion of units · 4. Dimension analysis.
2. **Concepts/definitions:** *Fundamental quantities* — "not expressed in terms of other physical quantities" (length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, luminous intensity, amount of substance). *Derived quantities* — defined in terms of the fundamental quantities (volume, velocity, density, acceleration, energy, power). SI base units: m, kg, s, A, K, cd, mol (with definitions, BK p.14–15).
3. **Laws/equations:** prefixes n 10⁻⁹, μ 10⁻⁶, m 10⁻³, c 10⁻², d 10⁻¹, da 10¹, k 10³, M 10⁶, G 10⁹ (Table 1.1). Dimensions: [ρ]=ML⁻³, [v]=LT⁻¹, [a]=LT⁻², [F]=MLT⁻², [W]=ML²T⁻², [P]=ML²T⁻³ (Table 1.2).
4. **Symbols:** [M] mass, [L] length, [T] time.
5. **Conditions:** dimensional homogeneity: both sides of a valid equation must have the same dimensions; dimensional analysis cannot find dimensionless constants (C = 2π found "in practice", BK p.19).
6. **Relations:** conversion factor × value; T ∝ √(l/g) derived by dimensions (Ex 1.4).
7. **Figures:** Fig 1.1 SI wheel; Fig 1.2 kilogram prototype.
8. **Examples:** 1.1 (90 km/h → 25 m/s) · 1.2 (13.6 g/cm³ → 1.36×10⁴ kg/m³) · 1.3 (verify T = 2π√(L/g)) · 1.4 (derive pendulum period) + slide examples (20 min→1200 s, 1800 s→30 min, 5 μm, 50 km).
9. **Common confusions (🧩):** cm³→m³ needs (10⁻²)³ not 10⁻²; confusing m (milli) with M (mega); thinking dimensional analysis proves the numeric constant.
10. **Skills:** convert units with prefixes, compound-unit conversion, check/derive equations with dimensions. Exercises 1–10 BK p.20–21.

### Chapter 2 — Vectors (S02, BK p.25–37)
1. **Sections:** 1. Scalar and vector quantities · 2. Vectors addition · 3. Vectors analysis · 4. Vectors product.
2. **Definitions:** scalar = magnitude only; vector = magnitude and direction. Vector properties: equality, commutative, associative, inverse, subtraction, multiplication by scalar.
3. **Equations:** C = √(A²+B²−2AB cos θ) (cosine rule, θ = angle inside triangle) · sine rule sin α/A = sin β/B = sin θ/C · right-angle: C = √(A²+B²), θ = tan⁻¹(B/A) · Aₓ = A cos θ, A_y = A sin θ · A + B by components · A·B = AB cos θ = AₓBₓ+A_yB_y+A_zB_z · A×B = AB sin θ n̂ = determinant · î·î=1, î·ĵ=0, î×ĵ=k̂, ĵ×k̂=î, k̂×î=ĵ.
4. **Symbols:** î, ĵ, k̂ unit vectors; n̂ unit normal (right-hand rule).
5. **Conditions:** components formula with θ measured from +x axis; scalar product commutative, vector product **not** (A×B = −B×A).
6. **Relations:** A ⟂ B ⇔ A·B = 0; A ∥ B ⇔ A×B = 0.
7. **Figures:** 2.2 vectors addition, 2.3 right-triangle addition, 2.4–2.8 properties, 2.9 components, 2.10 unit vectors, 2.11 scalar product, 2.12 right-hand rule.
8. **Examples:** 2.1 (4E+4N → 5.7 at 45°) · 2.2 (6 and 9, 120° → 7.9, β = 80.6°) · 2.3 (3 cm at 60° → 1.5î, 2.6ĵ) · 2.4 (7î+9ĵ) · 2.5 (1000) · 2.6 (140) · 2.7 (angle, see G-05) · 2.8 (1732 n̂) · 2.9 (book: 17k̂; computed k̂, see G-27).
9. **Confusions (🧩):** adding magnitudes directly; using the wrong angle in cosine rule; mixing sin/cos for components; forgetting the minus sign of the ĵ term in the determinant.
10. **Skills:** resolve, add/subtract by components, dot and cross products, angle between vectors. Exercises 1–10 BK p.36–37.

### Chapter 3 — Motion in one dimension (S03, BK p.41–53)
1. **Sections:** 1. Displacement · 2. Velocity · 3. Acceleration · 4. Motion with constant acceleration (+ Freely falling objects).
2. **Definitions:** displacement (vector, depends on start & end) vs distance (scalar, path length); average speed s = d/Δt; average velocity v_av = Δx/Δt; instantaneous velocity v = dx/dt; average acceleration a_av = Δv/Δt; instantaneous a = dv/dt.
3. **Equations:** (3.6) v_f = at + v_i · (3.7) x_f = ½at² + v_i t + x_i · (3.8) v_f² = v_i² + 2aΔx · free fall with g = 9.8 m/s²; y_max = v_i²/2g; t_max = v_i/g; flight time = 2 t_max.
4. **Symbols:** x position (m), t (s), v (m/s), a (m/s²), g (m/s²).
5. **Conditions:** equations 3.6–3.8 only when **a is constant**; free fall neglects air resistance; sign convention must be fixed (Ex 3.5 takes down +, Ex 3.6 takes up +).
6. **Relations:** v = dx/dt, a = dv/dt (polynomial differentiation).
7. **Figures:** 3.1 distance vs displacement; v–t and x–t graphs for constant a; free-fall diagrams (Ex 3.5, 3.6).
8. **Examples:** 3.1 (distance vs displacement) · 3.2 (x = 6t² − t + 1) · 3.3 (x = 2t³ − 3t² + 2t, a(3) = 30) · 3.4 (car from rest, a = 2.5) · 3.5 (thrown down 60 m/s) · 3.6 (thrown up 60 m/s from 210 m).
9. **Confusions (🧩):** speed vs velocity; negative acceleration ≠ slowing down (BK p.45); using constant-a equations for a variable a; mixing sign conventions.
10. **Skills:** compute displacement/distance; differentiate x(t); choose the right kinematic equation; free-fall problems. Exercises 1–10 BK p.52–53.

### Chapter 4 — Laws of Motion (S04, BK p.57–67)
1. **Sections:** 1. Force · 2. Newton's first law · 3. Newton's second law · 4. Newton's third law.
2. **Definitions:** force is a vector; contact vs field forces; Newton's 1st law: "In the absence of external forces, an object at rest remains at rest and an object in motion continues in motion with a constant velocity." Inertia; mass vs weight. 2nd law: "acceleration … directly proportional to the net force … and inversely proportional to its mass." 3rd law: "Every action has an equal and opposite reaction."
3. **Equations:** ΣF = 0 (equilibrium) · ΣF = ma · 1 N = 1 kg·m/s² · F₁₂ = −F₂₁ · W = mg.
4. **Symbols:** F (N), m (kg), a (m/s²), n normal force, T tension.
5. **Conditions:** inertial frame; action–reaction act on **different** bodies.
6. **Relations:** components ΣFₓ = maₓ, ΣF_y = ma_y.
7. **Figures:** 4.1 spring stretched by forces; 4.2–4.3 F, m, a proportionality; 4.4 Sun–Earth; 4.5 man on roof.
8. **Examples:** 4.1 (ΣF = 30 N right) · 4.2 (F₃ = 5.8 N, F₂ = 11.6 N) · 4.3 (child on ice, a = 0.5 m/s²; direction see G-26) · 4.4 (m = 2.5 kg) · slide example (20 N at 30° on 5 kg → 4 m/s²) · 4.5 (n = 200 N). Quiz (2 MCQ) BK p.62.
9. **Confusions (🧩):** action-reaction cancelling each other (they don't, different bodies); mass vs weight; normal force always = mg (not on an incline/elevator).
10. **Skills:** free-body diagrams, equilibrium, ΣF = ma in components, apparent weight. Exercises 1–10 BK p.66–67.

### Chapter 5 — Work and Energy (S05, BK p.71–83)
1. **Sections:** 1. Work · 2. Kinetic energy · 3. Potential energy · 4. Work and energy theory · 5. Power.
2. **Definitions:** work = energy transfer via a force; KE = ability to do work due to motion; PE due to position; power = rate of work.
3. **Equations:** W = F·Δr = ΔrF cos θ (J = N·m) · KE = ½mv² · W = ΔKE · ΔPE = mgΔh · W = ΔKE + ΔPE · P = dW/dt (W = J/s). Also 1 erg = 10⁻⁷ J, 1 eV = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ J (BK p.72).
4. **Symbols:** θ angle between F and Δr.
5. **Conditions:** constant force for W = FΔr cos θ; perpendicular force does zero work; PE reference level arbitrary, ΔPE is not.
6. **Relations:** sign of W from cos θ.
7. **Figures:** 5.1 car force directions; 5.2 work by force; 5.3 horse cart; 5.4 weightlifter; 5.5 energy forms.
8. **Examples:** 5.1 (4 J) · 5.2 (depth 10 m) · 5.3 (1000 J) · 5.4 (200 J) · 5.5 (20 J, KE_f — see G-07) · 5.6 (−240 000 J) · 5.7 (39.2 J, −78.4 J) · 5.8 (60 J, 39.2 J, v = 4.56 m/s) · 5.9 (see G-08) · 5.10 (58.8 J, 11.76 W).
9. **Confusions (🧩):** work done by perpendicular force; negative work; mixing W (work) with W (watt) symbol.
10. **Skills:** compute work (scalar & vector form), apply work–energy theorem, power. Exercises 1–10 BK p.82–83.

### Chapter 6 — Elasticity and Fluid Mechanics (S06, BK p.87–101)
1. **Sections:** 1. Elasticity · 2. Density · 3. Pressure (+ gauges) · 4. Fluid flow.
2. **Definitions:** stress = F/A (N/m²); strain = ΔL/L (unitless); Young's modulus Y = stress/strain; bulk modulus B; shear modulus S; elastic limit, yield point, breaking point (Fig 6.2); density ρ = m/V; pressure P = F/A (Pa); Pascal's law; ideal fluid properties (incompressible, non-viscous, steady, irrotational).
3. **Equations:** F/A = Y ΔL/L · F/A = −B ΔV/V · F/A = S x/L · ρ = m/V · P = ρgh · P = P₀ + ρgh · f/a = F/A · barometer P₀ = ρgh · R = Av · A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ · P₁ + h₁ρg + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + h₂ρg + ½ρv₂².
4. **Symbols:** Y, B, S (N/m²); R flow rate (m³/s).
5. **Conditions:** Hooke region (below elastic limit); Bernoulli for ideal fluid along a streamline.
6. **Relations:** larger area → lower flow speed; static fluid ⇒ P₁ − P₂ = (h₂ − h₁)ρg.
7. **Figures:** 6.1 wire under tension; 6.2 stress–strain curve; 6.3 bulk; 6.4 shear; 6.5 liquids of different density; 6.6 liquid column; 6.7 hydraulic press; 6.8 barometer; 6.9 manometer; 6.10–6.11 flow tubes.

9. **Confusions (🧩):** using diameter as radius in area; gauge vs absolute pressure; Bernoulli: faster flow ⇒ lower pressure.
